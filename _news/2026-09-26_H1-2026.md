The first six months of 2026 have been busy in all the best ways: new publications, new collaborations, new ideas, and quite a bit of travelling between across Germany. Here is a brief wrap-up of some of the highlights.
<h2>New publications</h2>
H1 brought two publications from ongoing work on T cell physiology and engineering. Our new [Cell study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.03.047) provides detailed insights into the *in vivo* behaviour of stem-like memory CAR T cells in a first-in-human clinical trial, highlighting their potential to support durable responses in adoptive cell therapy.<br>
We also contributed to a [study on proteasome activity](https://doi.org/10.1158/0008-5472.CAN-25-1246) in T cells, investigating how enhancing the cell's capacity to cope with proteotoxic and oxidative stress can delay exhaustion and improve antitumor activity. Together, these publications touch on complementary aspects of T cell therapy — from understanding how therapeutic T cells behave in patients to exploring cellular mechanisms that can influence their function. 
<h2>From idea to startup: GeneNovate</h2>
One of the highlights “off the beaten path” was the [GeneNovate program](/news/2025/GeneNovate2026/), where I joined [a team from the University Hospital Cologne](https://innere1.uk-koeln.de/forschung/arbeitsgruppen-labore/labor-fuer-tumorgenetik-und-zellbiologie/) working on smarter CAR T cells. The project was selected for presentation at the final event in Munich, and the program offered a very different perspective on how ideas move from biomedical research towards startups, products and the broader healthcare system — a learning experience I hope to build on in the future.
<h2>CIMT 2026: taking the microbiome project on the road</h2>
At the [CIMT](https://www.cimt.eu/) Annual Meeting in Mainz, I had the opportunity to present our work on tumor-associated microbiota both as a poster and in a selected oral presentation. The project explores how local antibiotic treatment can disrupt microbiota-driven immunosuppression and ultimately improve antitumor immune control. The discussions in Mainz provided some very valuable feedback as we now look forward to the publication appearing online soon.
<h2>Building the Cancer Network Bonn</h2>
June also marked an important milestone for the Cancer Network Bonn, newly formed within the [Transdisciplinary Research Area Life and Health](https://www.uni-bonn.de/en/research-and-teaching/research-profile/transdisciplinary-research-areas/tra-3-life-and-health/leben?set_language=en). Its first in-person workshop brought together oncology researchers and immunologists from across the university's medical and natural-science faculties and turned out as a great opportunity to strengthen connections across institutions and disciplines on campus, and hopefully the starting point for many more collaborations to come. 
<h2>Looking ahead</h2>
So, quite a lot happened in six months — and most of it feels less like a conclusion than a starting point. With the lab in Bonn taking shape, new collaborations forming and projects moving towards publication, I am looking forward to seeing where the second half of 2026 takes us.
