The role of <i>N</i>-acetylaspartate in the brain is unclear. Here we used specific antibodies against <i>N</i>-acetylaspartate and immunocytochemistry of carbodiimide-fixed adult rodent brain to show that, besides staining of neuronal cell bodies in the grey matter, <i>N</i>-acetylaspartate labelling was present in oligodendrocytes/myelin in white matter tracts. Immunoelectron microscopy of the rat hippocampus showed that <i>N</i>-acetylaspartate was concentrated in the myelin. Also neuronal cell bodies and axons contained significant amounts of <i>N</i>-acetylaspartate, while synaptic elements and astrocytes were low in <i>N</i>-acetylaspartate. Mitochondria in axons and neuronal cell bodies contained higher levels of <i>N</i>-acetylaspartate compared to the cytosol, compatible with synthesis of <i>N</i>-acetylaspartate in mitochondria. In aspartoacylase knockout mice, in which catabolism of <i>N</i>-acetylaspartate is blocked, the levels of <i>N</i>-acetylaspartate were largely increased in oligodendrocytes/myelin. In these mice, the highest myelin concentration of <i>N</i>-acetylaspartate was found in the cerebellum, a region showing overt dysmyelination. In organotypic cortical slice cultures there was no evidence for <i>N</i>-acetylaspartate-induced myelin toxicity, supporting the notion that myelin damage is induced by the lack of <i>N</i>-acetylaspartate for lipid production. Our findings also implicate that <i>N</i>-acetylaspartate signals on magnetic resonance spectroscopy reflect not only vital neurons but also vital oligodendrocytes/myelin.
